OPINION / OPERATORS & REPAIR SHOPS

Break the Chains: Let Operators Choose Who Fixes Their Tractors

We run the equipment. We line up the work. We need repair shops that can get the machine back on the job. Kong’s position is simple: ownership should come with a practical path to repair.

Kong Tractors editorial · October 19, 2026

The operator pays for the stopped machine

A tractor does not earn its keep while it waits. The operator still has a job to finish, transport to arrange and a customer expecting progress. From our side of the cab, repair access belongs in the equipment decision alongside power, attachments and price. It deserves a straight answer before the purchase, not a surprise after a fault.

Break the chains. Open the repair choices.

We want a workable choice between the owner’s maintenance capability, an independent specialist and dealer support. Those options can complement one another. The test is whether the person doing the job can obtain the information, parts and tools needed for that machine, at terms the owner understands. A repair pathway should be something you can explain clearly, rather than a vague promise on a sales sheet.

A good shop needs more than a wrench

Modern equipment can bring mechanical, hydraulic, electrical and software questions into the same fault. An independent shop needs to know which systems its diagnostic tools actually cover, which procedures it can complete and when another provider is required. We want clear service information and practical diagnostic access. A laptop subscription that cannot perform the required task is not a complete answer to the operator’s problem.

Check what access actually includes

John Deere’s Canadian self-repair page states that Customer Service ADVISOR is available directly to customers and independent repair shops, as well as through dealerships. That is a specific published access route, not proof that every tool covers every machine or procedure. Ask about the exact model, hardware, subscription, supported functions and any additional steps before relying on a diagnostic promise. Our view is that access should be judged by what it lets a competent repairer do.

Put repairability on the buying checklist

Before committing to new or used equipment, ask where the operator and technical manuals come from, how parts are identified, what diagnostic access costs and which work requires specialist support. Ask about local shop coverage and transport. Get the answers for the machine you are buying. A useful comparison includes the support you can reach after the sale, not just the equipment standing in the yard today.

Give independent shops room to compete

For the shops in our equipment community, the opportunity is to be precise: what makes and systems do you work on, where is the workshop, and what can be handled in the field? Tell operators how an assessment starts and what information to bring. Nobody benefits from an all-makes, all-problems promise that the shop cannot deliver. Clear capability and an honest handover are stronger than a broad claim.

Keep the repair record with the machine

Operators can improve the handover by collecting the model, serial number, hours, maintenance history, displayed fault information and a plain description of what happened. Keep records of completed work and unresolved items. Pass information to the repairer without opening hazardous systems or repeatedly operating faulty equipment to reproduce a symptom. The aim is a useful diagnosis and a documented return-to-service decision.

Repair choice deserves responsible workmanship

Break the chains is our call for repair independence. Repairs still need competent people, manufacturer procedures and the right equipment. We are not asking shops to defeat safety systems, emissions controls or security protections. We are asking for a service environment in which a capable repairer can assess the problem and the owner can make an informed choice about the work.

The machine should have a future beyond the fault

Our position: choose equipment with a credible repair path. Support shops that explain their capabilities. Ask manufacturers and sellers for usable information and clear access terms. Keep the operator in the decision. We bought the machine to put it to work. Let’s make keeping it working part of the deal.

Own it. Operate it. Keep it working.
