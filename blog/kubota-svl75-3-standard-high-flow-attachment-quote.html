Kubota SVL75-3: get the hydraulic package into the attachment quote

The model name is the start of the conversation. Put the actual hydraulic package, tool requirements and handover details together before booking a powered attachment.

By Kong Journal · November 18, 2026

A powered attachment quote should identify more than a loader model. The machine's fitted hydraulic package and the tool's requirements need to be considered together. For the Kubota SVL75-3, the manufacturer's separate standard and high-flow figures make that distinction useful before a booking is agreed.

This guide uses current manufacturer product information for an existing model. The working examples and quotation checklist are original Kong editorial analysis. Kong has not tested the loader, confirmed local stock or established that a particular attachment is suitable.

Keep both flow figures attached to their labels

Kubota Canada's SVL75-3 specification page lists standard auxiliary flow at 19.2 gpm (72.7 L/min) and high auxiliary flow at 29.8 gpm (112.8 L/min). Treat those as separate package descriptions. Ask the supplier to identify the installed system on the actual machine being offered.

A model badge, an online listing or an attachment pictured on another machine does not confirm the proposed unit's package. Get the machine identification and included options onto the quote so the attachment supplier can review the exact combination.

KEEP THE LABEL WITH THE NUMBER

Standard: 19.2 gpm. High: 29.8 gpm. Confirm the actual package and the attachment manufacturer's requirements together.

Describe the powered tool as carefully as the loader

Provide the attachment make, exact model and operating documentation. Ask the responsible supplier to check its specified flow range, pressure requirements, connections, controls and any other required plumbing. Flow alone does not settle compatibility or establish the output you will achieve on a job.

Useful question: “Which hydraulic package is fitted, and can you confirm this attachment is supported on that exact machine?” Keep the answer with the booking. If the attachment and loader come from different providers, agree who owns that compatibility check.

Put optional equipment, installation and delivery on separate quote lines. That helps reveal a missing connection or control kit before the attachment reaches the site. Avoid treating a package name as proof that every hose, control or fitting is included.

Separate hydraulic requirements from load-handling ratings

The Canadian page lists rated operating capacity at 2,490 lb at 35% of tipping load and 3,557 lb at 50%. Keep those definitions visible when comparing a specification sheet and a proposal. They are different rating bases for the same model, not interchangeable descriptions of every task.

For a load-handling job, provide reliable load information and the intended attachment, working height and site conditions to the supplier. Neither an attachment's own rating nor a hydraulic flow figure establishes a safe load for the complete machine and tool. Use the actual equipment's operating information and a competent assessment.

Turn the work into a useful demonstration request

Explain the task, material and conditions you expect. A powered broom, a ground-engaging tool and a material-handling attachment raise different questions. Ask the provider to explain the configuration it proposes and what it expects the operator to check before use.

If a demonstration is arranged, record the exact tool, machine package and conditions used. Keep any remaining assumptions beside the quote rather than treating an attractive video as proof of job-specific suitability. No productivity rate, fuel saving or ownership-cost result is established by this guide.

Include handover, service and transport in the package

Agree who supplies a competent operator, the relevant manuals and the handover instruction. Record the delivered condition, hours, fault-reporting contact and return expectations. Ask which routine checks belong to the operator and which maintenance tasks remain with the provider.

For Fraser Valley work, give the provider the actual access and ground information, especially where a job includes soft areas or finished surfaces. Have transport planned around the complete machine, tool and any included equipment. A general brochure figure does not settle every transport arrangement or ground condition.

This article gives no instructions to alter hydraulic settings, couple pressurized lines or service the loader. Follow the supplied manuals and have a suitably qualified person handle work outside the operator's agreed scope.

THE QUOTATION CHECK

Exact machine → fitted hydraulic package → exact attachment → confirmed requirements → operator handover and support.
