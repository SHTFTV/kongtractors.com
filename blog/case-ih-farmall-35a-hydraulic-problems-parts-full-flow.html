Today’s tractor: Case IH Farmall 35A

Case IH introduced its latest Farmall 35A and 40A compact tractors on February 9, 2026. The announcement includes optional third-function loader control and stackable hydraulic remotes, alongside a factory-matched loader. Those options make the actual attachment and hydraulic configuration important details in a repair enquiry.[1]

For tractor owners across the Fraser Valley and Greater Vancouver—including Langley, Aldergrove and Abbotsford—the useful question is practical: what information helps a shop assess the problem and identify the right parts? This guide connects that preparation with Durand at Full Flow Hydraulics.

About “common failures”: the sources below describe general hydraulic fault patterns. They do not establish failure rates or recurring defects for the Farmall 35A. The parts discussed here are assessment areas, not a list of parts every 35A needs.

First, identify which 35A you own

Do not order from the model name alone. Send the tractor’s product identification number or serial plate, operating hours and the year shown in your paperwork. Include the loader and attachment identification plates, transmission type if known, and whether the problem affects the loader, rear linkage, remote circuit or an attachment.

The 2026 announcement is a product reference, not a parts catalogue for every tractor carrying the 35A name. Ask the parts supplier to confirm the applicable serial range and manual. This article intentionally supplies no universal fluid grade, filter number or service interval.

What the symptom can tell the technician

Cross Manufacturing’s troubleshooting guidance identifies several possible causes of slow or irregular operation: fluid condition, air, restrictions and component wear. A load that drops in neutral can involve cylinder seals, fittings or a control valve. These are diagnostic possibilities, not conclusions about your tractor.[2]

  • Slow movement: say which function is slow and whether it changes after warm-up.
  • Jerky movement: describe hesitation or pulsing and whether it began after an attachment change or service.
  • Drift or leakage: describe what you observed from a safe position. Do not conduct a suspended-load test.
  • Noise or a warning: record the display message and what was happening when it appeared. Do not keep working to demonstrate the fault.

The hydraulic parts worth discussing

Use this list to organise the enquiry, then let the assessment decide the order. ENZED’s hose and flow guidance highlights restrictions, damaged or mismatched connections and contamination as possible contributors to poor movement.[3]

01 / Specified fluid and hydraulic filter

Wrong fluid or a restricted filter can affect operation.[2] Supply service records; have the shop confirm the correct specification, filter fitment and whether routine service or further investigation is needed.

02 / Hoses, fittings and quick couplers

A damaged hose, restriction or unsuitable coupling can affect flow.[3] Show the attachment identity and existing connection markings. Ask for a correctly specified assembly; a similar-looking connector does not establish fitment.

03 / Cylinder seals and rod condition

Cross lists worn seals and rod damage among causes of external cylinder leakage.[2] Ask whether inspection supports a seal repair, rod work or replacement. Confirm the cylinder’s identity before selecting a kit.

04 / Control valve and hydraulic pump

Wear in these components is another possible cause of poor performance.[2] A slow loader alone does not identify the failed component. Ask what model-specific testing is needed before approving a replacement assembly.

Stop safely before inspection

Unpredictable hydraulic movement is a reason to stop work and keep others clear. Follow the tractor and attachment manufacturer’s shutdown, support and isolation instructions. Do not inspect connections under pressure, loosen a fitting to release pressure or search for a leak with your hand. Leave hydraulic testing and repair to someone equipped for the work.[3]

Send Durand a useful first message

For a Farmall 35A enquiry, a short, complete description is more useful than “I think it needs a pump.” Send existing photos and observations; do not restart unsafe equipment to obtain them.

Tractor: Case IH Farmall 35A, identification plate, hours and year.
Equipment: loader, attachment and their identification details.
Problem: affected function, when it started, cold or warm behaviour, visible leak or warning.
History: recent work, fluid/filter records and any attachment change.
Location: your town and when the machine needs to return to work.

Before approving parts, confirm the diagnosis, part number and serial-range fitment, repair options, sourcing arrangements, estimate and expected timing. Full Flow confirms the work it can accept and whether further specialist or manufacturer support is required. For warranty questions, contact the appropriate Case IH dealer before authorising work.
