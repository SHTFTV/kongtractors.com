Ditch Witch SK800: plan the gate, the route and the attachment

Before hiring a compact loader for a backyard job, measure the route and identify the exact track and attachment setup. The SK800 brochure shows why those details matter.

By Kong Journal · November 13, 2026

A backyard project can depend on the few metres between the street and the work. A gate may be the narrowest point, but a corner, a low branch or the chosen attachment can become the real constraint. The Ditch Witch SK800 is a useful compact-machine example for planning that route carefully.

Ditch Witch’s July 23, 2026 rental feature discusses compact loaders for residential landscaping and names the SK800 among the options. This guide builds on that dated source with the current SK800 brochure. It is an original Kong planning feature about existing equipment, not a new launch, rental-stock listing or firsthand machine test.

Match the width figure to the fitted tracks

The current manufacturer brochure lists a minimum machine width of 36 in (91 cm) with 7-inch tracks and a maximum width of 42 in (107 cm) with 9-inch tracks. Those labels belong together. “It is an SK800” does not establish which of those configurations the hire company will deliver.

Measure the actual clear opening with the gate fully open and note anything projecting into the route. Send photographs and measurements to the provider. A published machine width is not a promise of clearance: the attachment, conditions and room needed to manoeuvre must also be assessed.

MEASURE THE WHOLE ROUTE

Gate opening → narrow section → corner → overhead constraint → working area. Ask the provider to assess the complete fitted machine against those conditions.

Keep the attachment width on the booking

The SK800 product page lists a choice of attachments, including 36-, 44- and 52-inch buckets. The machine’s narrowest listed track configuration does not make every attachment that narrow. Confirm the exact attachment model and overall dimensions on the proposed package.

Useful handover question: “Which track setup and which tool are you sending, and can you confirm the complete combination suits these measurements?” Keep the response with the hire agreement. If a tool has to be moved separately or fitted on site, have the provider plan and demonstrate that work under the appropriate manuals.

A turning point deserves its own photograph. An opening that works for a straight pass does not establish there is room to change direction immediately beyond it. Give the supplier the route rather than a single gate dimension.

Plan the job sequence before selecting tools

Describe the desired result in ordinary terms: moving loose material, preparing a surface, handling a known load or digging a specific feature. Ask which attachments and competent operator are appropriate for those tasks, and which parts of the work require a separate assessment.

For example, a property owner may want material moved from the driveway and the working area left ready for landscaping. That is a planning brief, not proof that a bucket alone will complete the work. Put each agreed tool on the quote, with its delivery, changeover and return arrangements.

The manufacturer’s general rental discussion suggests that attachment changes can expand what a compact loader does. It does not establish a saving, output rate or suitability for your property. Treat those as questions for the actual provider rather than promises to a client.

Keep lift ratings separate from the day's load

The SK800 brochure lists 911 lb (413 kg) rated operating capacity, identified as 35% of tipping capacity. It lists machine weight at 2,920 lb (1,324 kg) without an attachment and with fluids full. A complete transport or load-handling plan needs the actual fitted equipment and the relevant operating information.

Give the provider reliable load details and explain where the material starts and ends. An attachment’s own rating, the machine’s tipping figure or a picture of another loader handling a similar object cannot establish a safe working combination. Ask the responsible supplier to confirm the proposed setup.

Build a return plan into a short hire

A short booking still needs a clear handover. Record the supplied condition and starting hours, the manuals and instruction provided, the fault-reporting contact and the agreed cleaning and return expectations. Ask which operator checks are expected and which service tasks belong to the provider.

For a wet Fraser Valley site, include photographs of soft ground, finished surfaces and any area you want protected. This guide does not establish ground bearing capacity, traction or a safe slope. If those conditions are uncertain, arrange an assessment before booking.
