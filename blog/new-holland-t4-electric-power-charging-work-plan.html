New Holland T4 Electric Power: plan the work, then the charge

Kong’s electric-utility notebook: build a task and charging brief, separate assistance from autonomy, and ask for the current supported package.

By Kong Journal · November 2, 2026 · Sources checked November 2, 2026

An electric tractor changes the planning conversation before it changes the yard. For an owner, the interesting question is where the work happens, when the machine can pause and who can support the proposed equipment. The T4 Electric Power is a useful way to explore that question. This is an ownership-planning feature revisiting a historical manufacturer announcement, not a new product launch or a report of a Kong test.

The source, and the limits of the source

New Holland Agriculture North America’s August 28, 2023 release described the T4 Electric Power as an electric utility tractor with a 110 kWh maximum battery capacity, 74 hp rated output and a separately stated 65 hp PTO rating. It discussed AC and DC charging and assistance features including Follow Me and a camera-based bucket view. Those are descriptions from that dated release. Its launch target was 2024; a past target does not establish what can be ordered or supported in British Columbia today. Read the original manufacturer release.

We have not established current Canadian sales, delivery, configured charging requirements or feature availability. Ask New Holland or a verified authorised supplier for the current written offer. A model name in an old announcement is a research lead, not an inventory record. Kong makes no runtime, fuel-saving, reliability or emissions-lifecycle comparison.

Make a task sheet before asking for a runtime

Write down a representative day in the order it actually happens. Separate travelling, attachment work, loading and waiting. Note which tasks must happen at a fixed time and which can move. A single total-hours figure hides the difference between continuous demanding work and short jobs separated by long stops. This worksheet is Kong’s suggested enquiry method; it does not predict the T4’s energy use.

For a hypothetical acreage, the brief might say: morning material handling near the yard, a separate afternoon attachment job and an overnight parking period. Add the real implement identities, the conditions you expect and the supplier’s unanswered questions. Ask how a demonstration could represent that work and what evidence the supplier would record. Avoid turning a short demonstration into a claim about a full season.

THE JOB COMES FIRST.

Record the task, the implement and the available stopping windows.

Treat charging as part of the proposed installation

Give the supplier the proposed parking location and the time windows available. Ask for the exact charging equipment, connection requirements and site assessment needed for the quoted machine. Keep the charger, installation work and any unresolved site requirements in the same discussion. A charging-time headline without its equipment and conditions leaves a large part of the purchasing decision unanswered.

Request a written explanation of how the supplier would assess your proposed schedule and what happens if the normal charging window is missed. Include any remote work location in the brief. Do not assume a convenient socket is compatible or that a different market’s charging arrangement applies. Obtain the current equipment instructions and qualified site advice before committing to the installation.

Ask what an assistance feature actually permits

Separate each proposed function from the broad word autonomy. Ask the authorised supplier which functions are included in the exact package, where they may be used, what training is required and what the operator must do. Record any excluded task or condition beside the feature name. A named assistance mode does not establish permission for unattended work.

Electric utility notebook / 02

SUPPORT BELONGS IN THE PLAN.

Confirm the feature boundaries, the service provider and the next step when work stops.

Split the support questions clearly

Ask who supports the electric drivetrain, battery, charging equipment, software, tractor and attachments, and how the handover works when the cause is uncertain. Keep equipment identifiers and the applicable service documents together. For a later service enquiry, describe the observed symptom and the work preceding it rather than supplying an assumed diagnosis.

Leave the enquiry with a usable decision record

The useful result is a documented proposed package: actual tasks, a charging arrangement subject to site confirmation, clear feature boundaries and named support contacts. Mark unanswered questions openly. An electric utility tractor is an interesting design direction; deciding whether a particular offer suits your work takes evidence about that offer.
