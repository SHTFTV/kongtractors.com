Why Does My Gas Fireplace Pilot Light Keep Going Out?

November 24, 2026

A gas fireplace pilot light that keeps going out is most often caused by a worn, dirty or out-of-position thermocouple or thermopile, a partly clogged pilot orifice or dirty pilot assembly, drafts reaching the pilot, a gas supply or pressure problem, or a venting issue. The pilot system is built to fail safe: when the sensor doesn't detect enough heat from the flame, the gas valve closes. So a pilot that won't stay lit is usually a symptom, not the problem itself. Technical Safety BC lists pilot light outages among the signs that a gas appliance needs service right away, and it says gas appliance servicing should be done by a certified gas fitter working for a licensed gas contractor.

Here's what each cause looks like, what you can safely check yourself, and what to leave to a licensed technician.

How the pilot system works

Most gas fireplaces with a standing pilot use one or both of these sensors:

  • Thermocouple. A small sensor sitting in the pilot flame. When it's hot, it generates a tiny current that holds the gas valve open. If the flame goes out or weakens, the valve closes.
  • Thermopile. A bundle of thermocouples that produces more voltage, enough to run the valve and a wall switch or remote on many millivolt systems.

InspectAPedia, a long-running home inspection reference, describes these controls as designed to fail safe: the sensor keeps the gas valve open only while it's sensing heat from an adequate flame. If anything weakens that signal, the pilot goes out.

The most common causes

1. A worn, dirty or misaligned thermocouple or thermopile

Sensors wear out with years of heating and cooling, and soot or oxidation can coat them. A sensor that has shifted slightly out of the flame can also lose signal. A home inspection guide on gas fireplace pilots notes that a worn, dirty or poorly positioned thermocouple or thermopile is one of the most common reasons a pilot lights while the knob is held but goes out when released.

Technicians test thermopile output with a millivolt meter. Manufacturers publish minimum readings in their service manuals; one FMI fireplace manual, for example, lists 325 millivolts minimum for that model and says to replace the thermopile if the reading is lower.

2. A dirty or clogged pilot orifice

The pilot orifice is tiny. Dust, spider webs (common after a summer off) or debris can shrink the flame so it doesn't fully wrap the sensor. InspectAPedia advises checking for debris in the pilot orifice or tubing before replacing a thermocouple. A weak, lazy or yellow pilot flame is a clue.

3. Drafts

On older or non-sealed units, a draft from a nearby door, window or air return can push the flame away from the sensor. Angi's guide on pilot lights lists drafts near the appliance as a common cause, and notes pilots can blow out during storms. Sealed direct-vent units are less prone to room drafts but can still be affected by wind at the vent termination.

4. Gas supply or pressure problems

If the appliance isn't getting enough gas, the pilot won't hold. Angi notes that low gas supply can keep a pilot from staying lit. If other gas appliances are also acting up, mention it when you book service.

5. Venting or safety sensor issues

Blocked or damaged venting can starve the pilot of air or trip a safety sensor. InspectAPedia notes that on some units, an oxygen depletion sensor that detects unsafe conditions can cause the flame to go out. That's a sign to stop using the fireplace and get it checked, not to keep relighting.

If the pilot keeps going out, stop using the fireplace and book service with a certified gas fitter working for a licensed gas contractor. Leave the sealed glass and the gas controls alone.

When to stop and call

Technical Safety BC's bulletin on annual servicing lists signs that service is needed immediately, including:

  • Pilot light outages, or a yellow or wavering burner flame.
  • A continuous or intermittent gas odour.
  • Booming noises when the appliance starts.
  • Condensation on the inside of windows.

If you smell gas at any point, stop, get everyone outside and call FortisBC's emergency line or 911 from outside.

Prevention

Most repeat pilot problems are caught during routine maintenance. Technical Safety BC's checklist for gas fireplace servicing includes inspecting the gas valve, gas components and pilot system, checking the burner and air openings, and checking the entire vent system and termination. It also recommends a carbon monoxide detector for added protection.

If you live in a strata building where fireplaces are serviced on a building-wide schedule, Strata Property Services explains how councils organize that work.

The bottom line

A pilot that keeps going out is the safety system telling you something is off, most often a tired sensor, a dirty pilot, drafts, gas supply or venting. Check the simple things, don't force repeated relights, and book a licensed gas fitter, especially before the coldest weeks of winter.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my gas fireplace pilot light go out after I let go of the knob?

That usually means the thermocouple or thermopile isn't sensing enough heat to hold the gas valve open. The sensor may be worn, dirty or out of position, or the pilot flame may be too weak.

Is it dangerous if my fireplace pilot keeps going out?

The safety system is designed to close the gas valve when the flame goes out. Still, Technical Safety BC lists pilot outages as a sign that service is needed right away, so have a licensed gas fitter check it.

Can I replace a fireplace thermocouple myself in BC?

If the pilot repeatedly goes out, stop using the fireplace and have a certified gas fitter inspect it. If you smell gas, leave immediately and call the utility or 911 from outside.

Can wind blow out a gas fireplace pilot?

It can. Drafts in the room can affect older units, and strong wind at the outside vent can affect some direct-vent fireplaces. Check that the vent isn't blocked and mention the weather when you book service.
